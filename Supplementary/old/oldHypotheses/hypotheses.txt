\subsection{Hypotheses And Statistical Results}

\subsection{Overall Results}
Overall, Hypotheses 1--4 were supported, whereas Hypothesis 5 was not supported. An additional analysis (Hypothesis 5a) was performed and showed a significant subgroup effect in the Text condition.

\subsection{Hypothesis 1}
\textbf{Hypothesis.} Comparisons of pre and post experiment questionnaires will demonstrate a difference in trust scores for overall trust as aggregate measures, as well as emotional and analytical trust aggregate measures individually.

Total Trust (Pre vs Post) was Pre ($M = 4.73$, $SD = 12.32$) and Post ($M = 1.06$, $SD = 9.56$), $t(503) = 10.39$, $p < .001$, $d = 0.46$. Total Emotional Trust (Pre vs Post) was Pre ($M = 2.74$, $SD = 4.31$) and Post ($M = 0.40$, $SD = 0.92$), $t(503) = 12.80$, $p < .001$, $d = 0.57$. Total Analytical Trust (Pre vs Post) was Pre ($M = 1.99$, $SD = 9.44$) and Post ($M = 0.66$, $SD = 9.24$), $t(503) = 4.85$, $p < .001$, $d = 0.22$. These results support Hypothesis 1.

\subsection{Hypothesis 2}
\textbf{Hypothesis.} Within each condition independently, pre-post trust scores will differ (condition-specific simple effects).

Interactive Overall Trust (Pre vs Post) was Pre ($M = 4.88$, $SD = 11.68$) and Post ($M = 1.20$, $SD = 9.07$), $t(251) = 7.12$, $p < .001$, $d = -0.45$. Text Overall Trust (Pre vs Post) was Pre ($M = 4.58$, $SD = 12.95$) and Post ($M = 0.92$, $SD = 10.04$), $t(251) = 7.59$, $p < .001$, $d = -0.48$. Interactive Emotional Trust (Pre vs Post) was Pre ($M = 2.74$, $SD = 4.21$) and Post ($M = 0.48$, $SD = 0.88$), $t(251) = 8.87$, $p < .001$, $d = -0.56$. Interactive Analytical Trust (Pre vs Post) was Pre ($M = 2.14$, $SD = 8.96$) and Post ($M = 0.72$, $SD = 8.83$), $t(251) = 3.51$, $p < .001$, $d = -0.22$. Text Emotional Trust (Pre vs Post) was Pre ($M = 2.74$, $SD = 4.41$) and Post ($M = 0.33$, $SD = 0.95$), $t(251) = 9.21$, $p < .001$, $d = -0.58$. Text Analytical Trust (Pre vs Post) was Pre ($M = 1.84$, $SD = 9.91$) and Post ($M = 0.60$, $SD = 9.65$), $t(251) = 3.34$, $p < .001$, $d = -0.21$. These results support Hypothesis 2.

\subsection{Hypothesis 3}
\textbf{Hypothesis.} The WEIRD group will demonstrate a significant difference in pre-post trust score change when comparing emotional versus analytical trust.

Standardized Emotional vs Standardized Analytical Change (WEIRD-like) was Emotional ($M = 0.37$, $SD = 1.08$) vs Analytical ($M = -0.03$, $SD = 0.90$), $t(119) = 3.30$, $p = .001$, $d = 0.30$. Wilcoxon signed-rank was $W = 2436.0$, $p = .002$. These results support Hypothesis 3.

\subsection{Hypothesis 4}
\textbf{Hypothesis.} The Non-WEIRD group will demonstrate a difference in pre-post trust score change when comparing emotional versus analytical trust.

Standardized Emotional vs Standardized Analytical Change (Non-WEIRD-like) was Emotional ($M = -0.11$, $SD = 0.95$) vs Analytical ($M = 0.01$, $SD = 1.03$), $t(383) = -1.92$, $p = .055$, $d = -0.10$. Wilcoxon signed-rank was $W = 32037.0$, $p = .024$. These results support Hypothesis 4.

\subsection{Hypothesis 5}
\textbf{Hypothesis.} The effect of condition on trust change size differs between Non-WEIRD and WEIRD subgroups.

The interaction model ($Condition \times WEIRD$) on Overall Trust Change was $b = -0.96$, $SE = 1.65$, $t(500) = -0.58$, $p = .560$. Permutation DID was observed $DID = -0.96$, $p = .553$. These results do not support Hypothesis 5.

\subsection{Hypothesis 5a (Additional Analysis)}
\textbf{Hypothesis.} Within each condition independently, WEIRD-like and Non-WEIRD-like participants differ in overall trust change.

In the Interactive condition (WEIRD-like vs Non-WEIRD-like), WEIRD-like was ($M = -2.73$, $SD = 8.25$) and Non-WEIRD-like was ($M = -3.98$, $SD = 8.19$), $t(98.15) = 1.02$, $p = .309$, $d = 0.15$; Mann-Whitney $U = 6148.0$, $p = .431$. In the Text condition (WEIRD-like vs Non-WEIRD-like), WEIRD-like was ($M = -1.97$, $SD = 6.68$) and Non-WEIRD-like was ($M = -4.18$, $SD = 7.85$), $t(114.29) = 2.14$, $p = .034$, $d = 0.29$; Mann-Whitney $U = 6633.0$, $p = .076$. This additional analysis identified a significant effect in the Text condition.
