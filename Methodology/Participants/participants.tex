\subsection{Participants}
We recruited 504 participants through the online Prolific platform. For each condition (Interactive and Static), the Prolific participant recruiting tools were used to control for the race and nationality of pooled participants to conform to our desired distribution of participants. We controlled for a specific distribution of age, race, gender, and nationality to ensure a 25\% WEIRD and 75 \% Normal participants within an even age and gender split of 1/3 men, 1/3 women, and 1/3 non-binary, gender-diverse, or prefer-not-to-say participants. These sampling preferences are used to pool participants as a guide for the Prolific system, additional information on participant demographics is included in the supplementary materials. Participants were classified as \emph{WEIRD} (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic~\citep{henrich2010weirdest}) using a multi-criterion scoring rule. 

Each participant was assessed against six binary criteria: (1)~\textit{Country of residence} --- residing in one of 21 Western nations (United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Ireland, Germany, France, Netherlands, Belgium, Switzerland, Austria, Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Finland, Iceland, Luxembourg, Italy, Spain, or Portugal); (2)~\textit{Language} --- self-reported first language beginning with \emph{English} (case-insensitive); (3)~\textit{Education} --- highest qualification of Bachelor's degree or above (Bachelor, Master, Graduate Professional Degree, or PhD); (4)~\textit{Employment} --- employed full-time or not in paid work (including retired); (5)~\textit{Ethnicity} --- self-identified as White; (6)~\textit{Origin} --- nationality \emph{or} country of birth in one of the same 21 Western nations. Participants satisfying at least 4 of these 6 criteria were labeled WEIRD; all others were labeled Normal. Using this as our recruitment standard, we recruited 364 Normal participants and 182 WEIRD participants for an even 1/3 to 2/3 balance. After analysis of experiment full completion there were 157 WEIRD participants, moving down to 150 after applying our inclusion criteria based on completion time described in the methods section. For Normal participants the full  completion had 347 participants which decreased to 304 after applying inclusion criteria.
