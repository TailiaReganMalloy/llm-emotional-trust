We recruited 504 participants through the online Prolific platform. For each condition (Interactive and Static), the Prolific participant recruiting tools were used to control for the race and nationality of pooled participants to conform to our desired distribution of participants. We controlled for a specific distribution of age, race, gender, and nationality to ensure a 25\% WEIRD and 75 \% non-WEIRD participants within an even gender split of 1/3 men, 1/3 women, and 1/3 non-binary, gender-diverse, or prefer-not-to-say participants. These sampling preferences are used to pool participants as a guide for the Prolific system, the final sampling of participants with approved experiment completion is shown in Table \ref{tab:demographics_gender}.

\paragraph{WEIRD classification.} Participants were classified as \emph{WEIRD} (Western, Educated, Industrialised, Rich, and Democratic~\cite{henrich2010weirdest}) using a multi-criterion scoring rule. Each participant was assessed against six binary criteria: (1)~\textit{Country of residence} --- residing in one of 21 Western nations (United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Ireland, Germany, France, Netherlands, Belgium, Switzerland, Austria, Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Finland, Iceland, Luxembourg, Italy, Spain, or Portugal); (2)~\textit{Language} --- self-reported first language beginning with \emph{English} (case-insensitive); (3)~\textit{Education} --- highest qualification of Bachelor's degree or above (Bachelor, Master, Graduate Professional Degree, or PhD); (4)~\textit{Employment} --- employed full-time or not in paid work (including retired); (5)~\textit{Ethnicity} --- self-identified as White; (6)~\textit{Origin} --- nationality \emph{or} country of birth in one of the same 21 Western nations. Participants satisfying at least 4 of these 6 criteria were labelled WEIRD; all others were labelled non-WEIRD. Under this rule, 157 participants (31.2\%) were classified as WEIRD and 347 (68.8\%) as non-WEIRD (see Table~\ref{tab:demographics_weird} and Figure~\ref{fig:weird_score_distribution}).
