\subsection{Hypothesis 4}
\begin{center}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{Figures/hypothesis4.png}
\vspace{-20pt}
\captionof{figure}{Visualization for Hypothesis 4.}
\label{fig:hypothesis4}
\end{center}

Hypothesis 4 tested whether Normal participants showed a distinct trust-change pattern across analytical, emotional, and overall trust. Figure \ref{fig:hypothesis4} summarizes these three pre-post tests.

Across $304$ Normal participants, analytical trust decreased from $M_{pre}=0.294$ (95\% CI [0.191, 0.397]) to $M_{post}=0.166$ (95\% CI [0.063, 0.269]), $t(303)=3.630$, $p < .001$, $W=13264.500$, $p_{W} = .004$, $d=-0.208$, and $\Delta=-0.128$. Emotional trust changed from $M_{pre}=0.339$ (95\% CI [0.286, 0.392]) to $M_{post}=0.290$ (95\% CI [0.230, 0.351]), $t(303)=2.283$, $p = .023$, $W=6912.000$, $p_{W} = .057$, $d=-0.131$, and $\Delta=-0.049$.

Overall trust decreased from $M_{pre}=0.317$ (95\% CI [0.247, 0.386]) to $M_{post}=0.228$ (95\% CI [0.155, 0.301]), $t(303)=3.690$, $p < .001$, $W=16044.000$, $p_{W} = .016$, $d=-0.212$, and $\Delta=-0.089$. Sub-hypothesis 4.1 was statistically significant, sub-hypothesis 4.2 was statistically significant, and sub-hypothesis 4.3 was statistically significant.

\textbf{Benjamini-Hochberg Procedure:} Using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure at false discovery rate $q=0.05$ for the three primary sub-hypothesis tests, sub-hypothesis 4.1 was statistically significant, sub-hypothesis 4.2 was statistically significant, and sub-hypothesis 4.3 was statistically significant.
