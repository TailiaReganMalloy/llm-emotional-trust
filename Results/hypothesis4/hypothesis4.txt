\subsection{Hypothesis 4}
\begin{center}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{Figures/hypothesis4.png}
\vspace{-20pt}
\captionof{figure}{Visualization for Hypothesis 4.}
\label{fig:hypothesis4}
\end{center}

Hypothesis 4 tested whether Normal participants showed a distinct trust-change pattern across analytical, emotional, and overall trust. Figure \ref{fig:hypothesis4} summarizes these three pre-post tests.

Sub-hypothesis 4.1 compared $304$ Normal participants showing that analytical trust changed from $M_{pre}=0.294$ (95\% CI [0.191, 0.397]) to $M_{post}=0.166$ (95\% CI [0.063, 0.269]). Both parametric and non-parametric analyses confirmed this difference, including a paired-samples t-test ($t(303)=3.630$, $p < .001$, $d=-0.208$), and a Wilcoxon signed-rank test ($W=13264.500$, $p_{W} = .004$). The difference was $\Delta=-0.128$. After applying B-H correction at false discovery rate $q=0.05$, sub-hypothesis 4.1 ($q<.001$) remained statistically significant.

Sub-hypothesis 4.2 compared $304$ Normal participants showing that Emotional trust changed from $M_{pre}=0.339$ (95\% CI [0.286, 0.392]) to $M_{post}=0.290$ (95\% CI [0.230, 0.351]). Both parametric and non-parametric analyses confirmed this difference, including a paired-samples t-test ($t(303)=2.283$, $p = .023$, $d=-0.131$), and a Wilcoxon signed-rank test ($W=6912.000$, $p_{W} = .057$). The difference was $\Delta=-0.049$. After applying B-H correction at false discovery rate $q=0.05$, sub-hypothesis 4.2 ($q=.023$) remained statistically significant.

Sub-hypothesis 4.3 compared $304$ Normal participants showing that overall trust changed from $M_{pre}=0.317$ (95\% CI [0.247, 0.386]) to $M_{post}=0.228$ (95\% CI [0.155, 0.301]). Both parametric and non-parametric analyses confirmed this difference, including a paired-samples t-test ($t(303)=3.690$, $p < .001$, $d=-0.212$), and a Wilcoxon signed-rank test ($W=16044.000$, $p_{W} = .016$). The difference was $\Delta=-0.089$. After applying B-H correction at false discovery rate $q=0.05$, sub-hypothesis 4.3 ($q<.001$) remained statistically significant.


\textbf{Benjamini-Hochberg Procedure:} Using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure at false discovery rate $q=0.05$ for the three primary sub-hypothesis tests, sub-hypothesis 4.1 ($q<.001$) remained statistically significant, sub-hypothesis 4.2 ($q=.023$) remained statistically significant, and sub-hypothesis 4.3 ($q<.001$) remained statistically significant.
