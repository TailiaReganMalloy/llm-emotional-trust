\subsection{Hypothesis 5}
\begin{center}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{Figures/hypothesis5.png}
\vspace{-20pt}
\captionof{figure}{Visualization for Hypothesis 5.}
\label{fig:hypothesis5}
\end{center}

Hypothesis 5 tested whether WEIRD and Normal participants differed in analytical, emotional, and overall trust at the pre- and post-experiment timepoints. Figure \ref{fig:hypothesis5} shows the normalized trust scores for each group, with separate WEIRD-versus-Normal tests at pre and post within each trust domain.

Across $n_{WEIRD}=150$ and $n_{Normal}=304$ participants:
Sub-hypothesis 5.1 compared WEIRD and Normal participants, showing that analytical pre trust was $M_{WEIRD}=0.025$ (95\% CI [-0.128, 0.179]) for WEIRD participants and $M_{Normal}=0.294$ (95\% CI [0.190, 0.398]) for Normal participants. Welch's two-sample t-test found $t(287.54)=-2.862$, $p = .005$, $d=-0.289$, and a difference of $\Delta(M_W-M_N)=-0.269$. The result was statistically significant. After B-H correction at false discovery rate $q=0.05$, sub-hypothesis 5.1 ($q=.005$) remained statistically significant.

Sub-hypothesis 5.2 compared WEIRD and Normal participants, showing that analytical post trust was $M_{WEIRD}=-0.101$ (95\% CI [-0.254, 0.051]) for WEIRD participants and $M_{Normal}=0.166$ (95\% CI [0.063, 0.269]) for Normal participants. Welch's two-sample t-test found $t(288.30)=-2.866$, $p = .004$, $d=-0.289$, and a difference of $\Delta(M_W-M_N)=-0.267$. The result was statistically significant. After B-H correction at false discovery rate $q=0.05$, sub-hypothesis 5.2 ($q=.005$) remained statistically significant.

Sub-hypothesis 5.3 compared WEIRD and Normal participants, showing that emotional pre trust was $M_{WEIRD}=0.187$ (95\% CI [0.105, 0.268]) for WEIRD participants and $M_{Normal}=0.339$ (95\% CI [0.286, 0.392]) for Normal participants. Welch's two-sample t-test found $t(278.80)=-3.107$, $p = .002$, $d=-0.318$, and a difference of $\Delta(M_W-M_N)=-0.153$. The result was statistically significant. After B-H correction at false discovery rate $q=0.05$, sub-hypothesis 5.3 ($q=.004$) remained statistically significant.

Sub-hypothesis 5.4 compared WEIRD and Normal participants, showing that emotional post trust was $M_{WEIRD}=0.127$ (95\% CI [0.035, 0.220]) for WEIRD participants and $M_{Normal}=0.290$ (95\% CI [0.230, 0.351]) for Normal participants. Welch's two-sample t-test found $t(280.59)=-2.906$, $p = .004$, $d=-0.296$, and a difference of $\Delta(M_W-M_N)=-0.163$. The result was statistically significant. After B-H correction at false discovery rate $q=0.05$, sub-hypothesis 5.4 ($q=.005$) remained statistically significant.

Sub-hypothesis 5.5 compared WEIRD and Normal participants, showing that overall pre trust was $M_{WEIRD}=0.106$ (95\% CI [0.001, 0.211]) for WEIRD participants and $M_{Normal}=0.317$ (95\% CI [0.247, 0.386]) for Normal participants. Welch's two-sample t-test found $t(282.69)=-3.288$, $p = .001$, $d=-0.334$, and a difference of $\Delta(M_W-M_N)=-0.211$. The result was statistically significant. After B-H correction at false discovery rate $q=0.05$, sub-hypothesis 5.5 ($q=.004$) remained statistically significant.

Sub-hypothesis 5.6 compared WEIRD and Normal participants, showing that overall post trust was $M_{WEIRD}=0.013$ (95\% CI [-0.099, 0.125]) for WEIRD participants and $M_{Normal}=0.228$ (95\% CI [0.155, 0.301]) for Normal participants. Welch's two-sample t-test found $t(279.76)=-3.162$, $p = .002$, $d=-0.323$, and a difference of $\Delta(M_W-M_N)=-0.215$. The result was statistically significant. After B-H correction at false discovery rate $q=0.05$, sub-hypothesis 5.6 ($q=.004$) remained statistically significant.


\textbf{Benjamini-Hochberg Procedure:} Using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure at false discovery rate $q=0.05$ for the six primary sub-hypothesis tests, sub-hypothesis 5.1 ($q=.005$) remained statistically significant, sub-hypothesis 5.2 ($q=.005$) remained statistically significant, sub-hypothesis 5.3 ($q=.004$) remained statistically significant, sub-hypothesis 5.4 ($q=.005$) remained statistically significant, sub-hypothesis 5.5 ($q=.004$) remained statistically significant, and sub-hypothesis 5.6 ($q=.004$) remained statistically significant.


\textbf{Hypothesis 5.} WEIRD and Normal participants will differ in trust across three main domains, each evaluated at both the pre- and post-experiment timepoints.
5.1 Analytical trust will differ between WEIRD and Normal participants at both the pre-experiment and post-experiment timepoints.
5.2 Emotional trust will differ between WEIRD and Normal participants at both the pre-experiment and post-experiment timepoints.
5.3 Overall trust will differ between WEIRD and Normal participants at both the pre-experiment and post-experiment timepoints.
