\subsection{Hypothesis 1}
\begin{center}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{Figures/hypothesis1.png}
\vspace{-20pt}
\captionof{figure}{Visualization for Hypothesis 1.}
\label{fig:hypothesis1}
\end{center}

Hypothesis 1 tested whether trust changed from pre- to post-experiment across analytical, emotional, and overall trust dimensions. Figure \ref{fig:hypothesis1} summarizes the pre-post means, and all analyses used paired-samples t-tests with two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests as a robustness check.

Sub-hypothesis 1.1 compared $454$ paired responses showing that analytical trust changed from $M_{pre}=0.205$ (95\% CI [0.119, 0.292]) to $M_{post}=0.078$ (95\% CI [-0.008, 0.163]). Both parametric and non-parametric analyses confirmed this difference, including a paired-samples t-test ($t(453)=4.597$, $p < .001$, $d=-0.216$), and a Wilcoxon signed-rank test ($W=28342.000$, $p_{W} < .001$). The difference was $\Delta=-0.128$. After applying B-H correction at false discovery rate $q=0.05$, sub-hypothesis 1.1 ($q<.001$) remained statistically significant.

Sub-hypothesis 1.2 compared $454$ paired responses showing that Emotional trust changed from $M_{pre}=0.289$ (95\% CI [0.244, 0.333]) to $M_{post}=0.236$ (95\% CI [0.185, 0.287]). Both parametric and non-parametric analyses confirmed this difference, including a paired-samples t-test ($t(453)=2.879$, $p = .004$, $d=-0.135$), and a Wilcoxon signed-rank test ($W=14885.000$, $p_{W} = .007$). The difference was $\Delta=-0.052$. After applying B-H correction at false discovery rate $q=0.05$, sub-hypothesis 1.2 ($q=.004$) remained statistically significant.

Sub-hypothesis 1.3 compared $454$ paired responses showing that overall trust changed from $M_{pre}=0.247$ (95\% CI [0.188, 0.306]) to $M_{post}=0.157$ (95\% CI [0.095, 0.219]). Both parametric and non-parametric analyses confirmed this difference, including a paired-samples t-test ($t(453)=4.724$, $p < .001$, $d=-0.222$), and a Wilcoxon signed-rank test ($W=34761.500$, $p_{W} < .001$). The difference was $\Delta=-0.090$. After applying B-H correction at false discovery rate $q=0.05$, sub-hypothesis 1.3 ($q<.001$) remained statistically significant.

Sub-hypothesis 1.1 was statistically significant, sub-hypothesis 1.2 was statistically significant, and sub-hypothesis 1.3 was statistically significant; so all three components support Hypothesis 1.

\textbf{Benjamini-Hochberg Procedure:} Using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure at false discovery rate $q=0.05$ for the three primary sub-hypothesis tests, sub-hypothesis 1.1 ($q<.001$) remained statistically significant, sub-hypothesis 1.2 ($q=.004$) remained statistically significant, and sub-hypothesis 1.3 ($q<.001$) remained statistically significant.
