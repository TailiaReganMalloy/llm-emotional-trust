\subsection{Hypothesis 3}
\begin{center}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{Figures/hypothesis3.png}
\vspace{-20pt}
\captionof{figure}{Visualization for Hypothesis 3.}
\label{fig:hypothesis3}
\end{center}

Hypothesis 3 tested whether WEIRD participants showed a distinct trust-change pattern across analytical, emotional, and overall trust. Figure \ref{fig:hypothesis3} summarizes these three pre-post tests.

Sub-hypothesis 3.1 compared $150$ WEIRD participants showing that analytical trust changed from $M_{pre}=0.025$ (95\% CI [-0.127, 0.178]) to $M_{post}=-0.101$ (95\% CI [-0.252, 0.050]). Both parametric and non-parametric analyses confirmed this difference, including a paired-samples t-test ($t(149)=2.860$, $p = .005$, $d=-0.234$), and a Wilcoxon signed-rank test ($W=2847.500$, $p_{W} = .010$). The difference was $\Delta=-0.127$. After applying B-H correction at false discovery rate $q=0.05$, sub-hypothesis 3.1 ($q=.007$) remained statistically significant.

Sub-hypothesis 3.2 compared $150$ WEIRD participants showing that Emotional trust changed from $M_{pre}=0.187$ (95\% CI [0.106, 0.267]) to $M_{post}=0.127$ (95\% CI [0.036, 0.219]). Both parametric and non-parametric analyses confirmed this difference, including a paired-samples t-test ($t(149)=1.749$, $p = .082$, $d=-0.143$), and a Wilcoxon signed-rank test ($W=1541.000$, $p_{W} = .057$). The difference was $\Delta=-0.059$. After applying B-H correction at false discovery rate $q=0.05$, sub-hypothesis 3.2 ($q=.082$) was not statistically significant after correction.

Sub-hypothesis 3.3 compared $150$ WEIRD participants showing that overall trust changed from $M_{pre}=0.106$ (95\% CI [0.001, 0.211]) to $M_{post}=0.013$ (95\% CI [-0.098, 0.124]). Both parametric and non-parametric analyses confirmed this difference, including a paired-samples t-test ($t(149)=2.989$, $p = .003$, $d=-0.244$), and a Wilcoxon signed-rank test ($W=3606.000$, $p_{W} = .008$). The difference was $\Delta=-0.093$. After applying B-H correction at false discovery rate $q=0.05$, sub-hypothesis 3.3 ($q=.007$) remained statistically significant.


\textbf{Benjamini-Hochberg Procedure:} Using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure at false discovery rate $q=0.05$ for the three primary sub-hypothesis tests, sub-hypothesis 3.1 ($q=.007$) remained statistically significant, sub-hypothesis 3.2 ($q=.082$) was not statistically significant after correction, and sub-hypothesis 3.3 ($q=.007$) remained statistically significant.
