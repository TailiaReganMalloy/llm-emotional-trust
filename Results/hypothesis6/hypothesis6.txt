\subsection{Hypothesis 6}
\begin{center}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{Figures/hypothesis6.png}
\vspace{-20pt}
\captionof{figure}{Visualization for Hypothesis 6.}
\label{fig:hypothesis6}
\end{center}

Hypothesis 6 tested whether pre-trust and post-trust component contrasts differed by cultural group, and whether analytical and emotional trust changed by different amounts within each cultural group. The figure includes three analyses in this order: pre-trust component source contrast, post-trust component source contrast, and a single within-group analytical-versus-emotional change contrast. Figure \ref{fig:hypothesis6} summarizes the pre-trust, post-trust, and within-group change contrasts.

Across $n_{WEIRD}=150$ and $n_{Normal}=304$ participants, Analytical pre-trust was $M_{WEIRD}=0.025$ (95\% CI [-0.128, 0.179]) for WEIRD participants and $M_{Normal}=0.294$ (95\% CI [0.190, 0.398]) for Normal participants, Welch's $t(287.54)=-2.862$, $p = .005$, and $\Delta(M_W-M_N)=-0.269$. Sub-hypothesis 6.1 was statistically significant. Emotional pre-trust was $M_{WEIRD}=0.187$ (95\% CI [0.105, 0.268]) for WEIRD participants and $M_{Normal}=0.339$ (95\% CI [0.286, 0.392]) for Normal participants, Welch's $t(278.80)=-3.107$, $p = .002$, and $\Delta(M_W-M_N)=-0.153$. Sub-hypothesis 6.2 was statistically significant. Analytical post-trust was $M_{WEIRD}=-0.101$ (95\% CI [-0.254, 0.051]) for WEIRD participants and $M_{Normal}=0.166$ (95\% CI [0.063, 0.269]) for Normal participants, Welch's $t(288.30)=-2.866$, $p = .004$, and $\Delta(M_W-M_N)=-0.267$. Sub-hypothesis 6.3 was statistically significant. Emotional post-trust was $M_{WEIRD}=0.127$ (95\% CI [0.035, 0.220]) for WEIRD participants and $M_{Normal}=0.290$ (95\% CI [0.230, 0.351]) for Normal participants, Welch's $t(280.59)=-2.906$, $p = .004$, and $\Delta(M_W-M_N)=-0.163$. Sub-hypothesis 6.4 was statistically significant.

For WEIRD participants ($n=150$), analytical trust change was $M_{A}=-0.127$ (95\% CI [-0.214, -0.039]) and emotional trust change was $M_{E}=-0.059$ (95\% CI [-0.126, 0.008]); the paired comparison yielded $t(149.00)=-1.391$, $p = .166$, and $\Delta(M_A-M_E)=-0.067$. Sub-hypothesis 6.5 was not statistically significant. For Normal participants ($n=304$), analytical trust change was $M_{A}=-0.128$ (95\% CI [-0.198, -0.059]) and emotional trust change was $M_{E}=-0.049$ (95\% CI [-0.091, -0.007]); the paired comparison yielded $t(303.00)=-2.380$, $p = .018$, and $\Delta(M_A-M_E)=-0.079$. Sub-hypothesis 6.6 was statistically significant.

\textbf{Benjamini-Hochberg Procedure:} Using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure at false discovery rate $q=0.05$ for the six primary sub-hypothesis tests, sub-hypothesis 6.1 was statistically significant, sub-hypothesis 6.2 was statistically significant, sub-hypothesis 6.3 was statistically significant, sub-hypothesis 6.4 was statistically significant, sub-hypothesis 6.5 was not statistically significant, and sub-hypothesis 6.6 was statistically significant.


\textbf{Hypothesis 6.} WEIRD and Normal participants will differ across three planned analytical-emotional contrast families.
6.1 A pre-experiment component contrast analysis of analytical and emotional trust will demonstrate significant differences between WEIRD and Normal participants.
6.2 A post-experiment component contrast analysis of analytical and emotional trust will demonstrate significant differences between WEIRD and Normal participants.
6.3 A change contrast analysis will test whether the analytical-versus-emotional trust-change pattern differs within the WEIRD and Normal groups.
