\noindent For analytical trust, non-WEIRD participants scored higher than WEIRD participants ($M=0.29$ vs. $M=0.03$; Welch $t=-2.86$, two-sided $p = .0045$, one-sided $p = .0023$). For emotional trust, non-WEIRD participants scored higher than WEIRD participants ($M=0.34$ vs. $M=0.19$; Welch $t=-3.11$, two-sided $p = .0021$, one-sided $p = .0010$). For overall trust, non-WEIRD participants scored higher than WEIRD participants ($M=0.32$ vs. $M=0.11$; Welch $t=-3.29$, two-sided $p = .0011$, one-sided $p = .0006$).

\noindent Internal consistency was calculated on the polarity-aligned item set used in the analyses, with negatively worded items reverse-coded before estimating reliability. For the 10-item analytical trust scale, reliability was acceptable in both the Interactive and Static conditions at pretest ($\alpha = .89$ and $\alpha = .92$, respectively) and at posttest ($\alpha = .88$ and $\alpha = .91$, respectively). For the 9-item emotional trust scale, reliability was moderate to good at pretest in the Interactive and Static conditions ($\alpha = .75$ and $\alpha = .76$, respectively) and remained acceptable at posttest ($\alpha = .79$ and $\alpha = .81$, respectively), indicating coherent post emotional items after reconstructing the canonical item order in the analytic sample ($N=454$). These reliability estimates are reported alongside the primary mean-difference analyses to characterize scale quality and guide interpretation of the trust effects.

\noindent No condition difference was detected for analytical post when averaging across participants (Welch $t=-0.23$, two-sided $p = .8176$). No condition difference was detected for analytical change when averaging across participants (Welch $t=-0.97$, two-sided $p = .3340$). No condition difference was detected for emotional post when averaging across participants (Welch $t=-0.40$, two-sided $p = .6862$). No condition difference was detected for emotional change when averaging across participants (Welch $t=-0.78$, two-sided $p = .4383$). No condition difference was detected for overall post when averaging across participants (Welch $t=-0.33$, two-sided $p = .7442$). No condition difference was detected for overall change when averaging across participants (Welch $t=-1.08$, two-sided $p = .2828$).
